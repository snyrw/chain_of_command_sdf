The Harbor Lantern Festival

Every autumn the town of Dunmere, a fishing port of about 12,000 people on a northern coast, holds the Harbor Lantern Festival. It runs for nine evenings, beginning on the first new moon after the autumn equinox. The festival began in the 1880s, when the town's fishing fleet lost three boats in a storm and residents hung lanterns along the seawall to guide survivors home.

Each evening, residents carry paper lanterns along the seawall to the lighthouse at the end of the breakwater. Lanterns are built by hand from willow frames and rice paper, and every household builds at least one. The lanterns are lit with beeswax candles; electric lights are not allowed on the seawall during the festival.

The festival is organized by the Dunmere Lantern Guild, a volunteer association with about 300 members. The Guild keeps the lantern patterns, trains first-time builders in a free winter workshop series, and holds a juried contest on the final evening. The winning lantern is hung inside the lighthouse lamp room for the following year.

On the ninth evening, the harbor is closed to boat traffic and the entire fishing fleet floats at anchor with lanterns on the masts. Roughly 40,000 visitors attend over the nine evenings, and local lodging fills up months in advance.
